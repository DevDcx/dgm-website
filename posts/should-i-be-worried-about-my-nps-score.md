First, let's answer the questions. What is an NPS score? Why should I care when I'm getting reviews with ratings? How can I get my NPS score up?

## What is my NPS score?

NPS is the percentage of promoters minus the percentage of detractors among customers who rate you from 0 to 10. A promoter is someone who gives you a rating of 9 or 10. A detractor is someone who gives you a rating between 0 and 6. Those who rate 7 or 8 are considered passive. In other words, they neither promote nor detract from your brand reputation.

Your NPS score correlates most closely with brand affinity, which is the power of your brand to hold its own in a highly competitive environment. It is the strongest indicator that customers will choose you over your competitors.

## Why should I care when I'm getting reviews?

Promoters are talking about your business to their friends, family and colleagues and suggesting they check you out. They are brand advocates, the most powerful part of a customer acquisition strategy. Unpaid word of mouth is much more effective than trying to compete with paid ads in a crowded market.

Detractors are also talking about your business. In fact, a person who has had a bad experience is likely to tell 15 people not to choose your business, while a promoter is likely to tell 3 others to try it.

That's a scary fact. It's why it's so important to turn detractors into promoters and not just write them off.

How does that relate to 5-star review ratings from comment cards and social reviews? A review rating is connected to a specific experience, and may not correlate with your NPS rating.

A customer who gives you a high NPS score and is a brand advocate can have a bad experience without it changing how they feel about your brand. A negative review from a customer with a high NPS is a good thing. It shows they're motivated to tell you, so you can fix the problem. They really care about your brand and want you to succeed.

Of course, if they keep having bad experiences, they'll drop into the detractor category. They'll tell others how disappointed they are, how you used to be really good, but now they go somewhere else.

## How can I get my NPS score up?

It's not rocket science. Let's start from the bottom up.

### Turn detractors into promoters

Respond to poor experiences immediately, ideally before the customer leaves the business, or at least before they have a chance to rant on social media and amplify the damage.

1. Send automatic responses to customers and alert managers in real time for review ratings between 1 and 3, and NPS scores between 0 and 6.
2. Have the store manager investigate the problem and flag an action to resolve any internal issues causing poor customer experiences.
3. Contact the customer personally to apologize and thank them for alerting you to the problem. Negative feedback is a gift that helps you tune up your operations.
4. Identify with how they're feeling. Name the feeling (for example, "you must feel very frustrated"). This releases negative emotions so you can get to a resolution.
5. Let them know what you've done to resolve the issue, so they feel like they matter.
6. Ask them what would make it right for them. Often, if you've done steps 1 to 5, they'll just say "It's OK, never mind. I understand." If you miss one of those steps, they're more likely to stay angry, and nothing you offer will satisfy them.

### Acknowledge and affirm promoters

Companies are often so focused on reputation management and keeping bad reviews from going public that they forget about their most valuable customers: their brand advocates. Brand advocates come in more often and spend more. To keep them loyal, you need to stay top of mind and make them feel special. Here are actions you can take:

1. Thank them for good reviews and let them know you love them too.
2. Send personalized gifts or promotions that are relevant to them, like event invitations, new product announcements or offers on products they're interested in.
3. For customers with an above-average basket or spend, send periodic personal discounts on their favourite products to get them in one more time per period.
4. For customers who come in often with a low average basket or spend, send suggestions for new products.
5. For new customers, consider a welcome gift. For customers in the top 10% of sales, consider a gift each year.

### Find out how to move passives to promoters

Although passives aren't actively affecting your brand reputation, you have an opportunity to learn from them. Here's how.

1. Periodically run an email campaign that invites customers who scored 7 or 8 to tell you what you'd need to do to earn a 9 or 10.
2. Offer an incentive, like a chance to win, a gift card, or a discount on a product they like. Remember, they don't have a strong emotional reason to engage with you. They'll need an incentive.
3. Include a question that identifies the area that most needs attention, for example product, service or the physical store.
4. Act on their insights to up your game in whatever category is weakest. Share feedback with your teams and brainstorm how they would turn a mediocre experience into a great one.
