Maintaining excellent customer service in the face of rising labour costs, high turnover, and staff shortages is becoming increasingly challenging. This is especially true for operations managers in big box chains, who must balance customer service demands with staff efficiency in areas like warehousing, restocking, and checkout.

We've all heard stories of customers struggling to find help in stores spanning over 142,000 square feet. The footprint of the average big box store has increased by [about 12 per cent over the last decade](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2019-03-15/big-box-retail-struggling-as-shoppers-shift-to-online-sales/10900916). Customers may start to limit their visits, or avoid big box stores altogether, if finding help is too hard. So how do we deliver exceptional retail customer service in the face of staffing challenges?

It's not just customers who face frustrations. Associates also find it hard to handle constant customer requests while juggling their daily tasks. This can lead to less-than-welcoming service and lower productivity. Imagine how many kilometres a day the average associate walks on the retail floor to meet and help customers. What does that represent in cost per service request and lost productivity? Thankfully, there is a better way.

## Seven strategies retail operations and customer service leaders use to deliver exceptional service at a lower cost

### 1. Make customer service easy to reach with QR codes on signage

Let customers connect to associates for support through their phones. Offer them the choice to communicate how they're most comfortable: SMS, phone or video call. Follow up every service request with a comment card.

### 2. Resolve questions instantly with AI-powered chatbots

Many big box retailers have customer self-serve apps, but they're confusing and require a download. Make self-serve easier by adding chatbots to a web-based in-store app that guides customers to the right information. Always provide an option to connect with a live associate for personalized support.

### 3. Speed up stock checks with video calls

If customer self-serve stock checks aren't available, enable video calls for remote stock checks. Reduce customer wait times and drastically improve efficiency.

### 4. Ask for customer feedback at self-serve checkout

Retail customers are often asked to visit a website listed on the receipt to give feedback. With less than 1% of customers responding, it's hard to get a good understanding of your customers' in-store experience. You can get more than 25% of customers sharing insights in real time by collecting feedback during the payment process.

### 5. Alert managers to problems in real time

Most retail customer experience management systems are run from head office. Enable real-time problem resolution with manager alerts. Empower store managers to solve problems before they hurt customer satisfaction and NPS scores.

### 6. Get associate feedback on both customer and team experiences

Your associates always know more about your customers than you do. Why? Because your customers are speaking with them all day, every day. Happy employees create happy customers, and vice versa. Create opportunities for employees to share their insights and ideas through quick pulse checks and an idea board. Tap into their wealth of knowledge.

### 7. Use AI for performance benchmarking and KPI alerts

Data is everywhere, spread across many applications. There's too much of it to spot the most significant trends without help from technology. Consolidate your data into a single platform and set KPIs to track and improve operations. Use AI to generate reports and prioritize alerts, so decisions are well informed.

With these strategies, retailers can improve the experience of both customers and employees while making associates more productive. Breathe easier as an operations manager, knowing you're getting the best from your teams and stretching your labour budgets further.

Together, we can overcome staff shortages, high turnover, and rising labour costs and thrive in the retail industry.
